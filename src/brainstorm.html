What should we test?

Raise your heart rate with exercise, or lower it by calming down with meditation, breathing, and more. Your experiment needs two conditions to compare, because that is what makes the p-value work later. Steal an idea below or invent your own.

Ground rules for every experiment

  • NO VIDEO GAMES. Not as the thing you change, and not as the thing you measure.
  • No caffeine, energy drinks, or supplements this year.
  • Light to moderate exercise only. Stop right away if you feel dizzy, sick, or hurt.
  • Your teacher must approve your plan before you collect any data.
  • Keep health information private. Use first names or initials in anything you share.

Raise your heart rate

Exercise like jumping jacks, planks, and sit-ups. Measure right after you stop, then see how fast it comes back down.

Lower your heart rate

Calm down with breathing, meditation, relaxing sounds, or closing your eyes. Sit quietly for 2 minutes first, then measure your resting heart rate. Use the same seat and the same time of day for every trial.

Experiment builder

Pick one thing to change and one thing to measure. The builder tells you if they make a real experiment, then gives you a starting plan. Everything here works in an NYC apartment, a school hallway, or a playground.

Tools for measuring

PulseFind your pulse on your wrist or neck. Count for 15 seconds and multiply by 4.
Reaction timeUse the class reaction time test (milliseconds), or the ruler drop test with a partner (centimeters).
Percent changeFor heart rate, breaths, and running time, calculate (After − Before) ÷ Before × 100. See the Percent change page.
StopwatchUse the same phone stopwatch every time, and have the same person start and stop it.
Breaths per minuteA partner counts your breaths for 30 seconds and doubles it. Do not tell the person when counting starts.
