Tables and graphs in Google Sheets

A clean table and the right graph make your data easy to trust. Follow these steps in Google Sheets, then use the checklists before you put anything on a slide. If a menu looks a little different on your screen, look for the same words.

Three rules for every table and graph

  • UNITS: Every measurement needs its unit, like (bpm), (Seconds), or (ms). Put units in the column headers and the axis titles.
  • Capitalize Important Words: Capitalize all the important words in titles, headers, and axis titles. Keep small words like "of", "and", and "the" lowercase unless they come first.
  • Right graph for the data: Check that your graph type fits your data (see Part 2).
Part 1. Build your data table
  1. Open a new sheet. In Google Drive, click New, then Google Sheets.
  2. Type your headers in row 1. Capitalize all important words and put the unit in parentheses.
    Trial  |  Condition A: 30 Seconds of Jumping Jacks (bpm)  |  Condition B: 60 Seconds of Jumping Jacks (bpm)
  3. Type your data underneath. One number per cell. Type only the number, not the unit. The unit is already in the header.
  4. Make the headers stand out. Click the row 1 number on the left to select the row. Click Bold (B) in the toolbar, then the center align button.
  5. Add alternating colors. Select your whole table (click the first cell, hold Shift, click the last cell). Open the Format menu, then Alternating colors. Check "Header" and pick a color style. Click Done.
  6. Outline the cells. With the table still selected, click the Borders button in the toolbar (it looks like a small grid). Choose All borders.
  7. Add the averages. In the row under your data, type "Average" in the first column. In the next cell type =AVERAGE(B2:B6) and press Enter. Change B2:B6 to match your own cells.
  8. Add percent change (for heart rate, running time, and other measurements with a starting number). Make a column for Before and a column for After. In a new column, type =(C2-B2)/B2 (After minus Before, divided by Before) and press Enter. Then open Format, Number, Percent. Drag the corner of the cell down to fill the rest of the rows. Do not multiply by 100, because the percent format does that. Explanation on the P-value page.
  9. Tidy up. Center the numbers. Use the "decrease decimal places" button to round your averages. Double-click the line between two column letters to fit the column to the words.

Part 2. Pick the right graph

Ask yourself: what is on the bottom (x-axis) of my graph?

If the bottom shows...Use this graphExample
Separate groups or conditionsColumn chart (bar graph)Average heart rate for Slow Music vs Fast Music
Time passingLine chartHeart rate every 15 seconds after exercise
A number you changed on purposeScatter chartSeconds of jumping jacks (20, 40, 60, 80) vs heart rate
Parts of a wholePie chart (you will not need this one)Not for this experiment

Most students comparing Condition A to Condition B should use a column chart of the averages. Do not use a line chart to connect two separate groups.

Part 3. Make your graph
  1. Make a small summary table. One column for your conditions and one for the averages, with the same Capitalized headers and units.
  2. Select it, then insert the chart. Open Insert, then Chart. The Chart editor opens on the right.
  3. Check the chart type. On the Setup tab, open the Chart type dropdown and choose the type from Part 2 (usually Column chart).
  4. Add titles. Click the Customize tab, then Chart and axis titles. Use the dropdown to switch between titles, and type each one in Title Case:
    • Chart title: Average Heart Rate for Slow and Fast Music
    • Horizontal axis title: Type of Music
    • Vertical axis title: Average Heart Rate (bpm)
  5. Start the bars at zero. In Customize, open Vertical axis and set the Min value to 0, so the bars do not exaggerate small differences.
  6. Show the numbers. In Customize, open Series and check Data labels so each bar shows its value.
  7. Remove a legend you do not need. If you only have one data series, open Legend in Customize and set the position to None.
  8. Make the text big. Use the size options in each section so your titles and numbers can be read from the back of the room.
  9. Put it on your slide. Click the chart, click the three dots in its corner, and choose Copy chart. In Google Slides, paste it and pick Link to spreadsheet or Paste unlinked. (You can also download it as a PNG from the same menu.)
