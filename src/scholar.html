Sources: Google Scholar and APA

Real scientists build on what other scientists already found. For this project you will find scholarly sources on Google Scholar, explain how they connect to your experiment, and cite them in APA format.

Part 1. Find a source on Google Scholar
  1. Search with keywords, not a full question. Use the topic words from your experiment, like slow breathing heart rate. The starter searches below open Google Scholar for you.
  2. Set the date. On the left side of the results page, pick a recent range such as "Since" a year within the last 10 to 15 years. You can also click "Review articles" to get summaries of lots of research.
  3. Scan each result. Look at the title, the authors, and the year. Click the title to see the abstract, which is a short summary of the study.
  4. Look for a free copy. A [PDF] or [HTML] link on the right side is usually free to read. If there is none, click "All versions". The abstract alone is often enough for this project.
  5. Click "Cite". A box pops up with APA format. Copy it, but then fix it using the checklist in Part 3. Google's version is often not perfect.

  1. Title. Click it to read more.
  2. Authors, journal, and year. This is the info you need for your citation. The journal name is a good sign: real studies are published in journals.
  3. Snippet. A tiny piece of the paper that matched your search words.
  4. Free link. If it is there, use it.
  5. Tools. "Cite" makes a citation. "Related articles" finds similar studies. "Cited by" shows newer papers that used this one.

Part 2. Is this a good source? Is it useful?

Check each box that is true. You want most of them before you use a source.

Summarize it in two sentences

You do not need to understand every word. Read the title, the abstract, and the last paragraph. Then fill in this frame:

Summary: In Rivera and Chen (2019), researchers tested what they changed on what they measured and found what happened.
What this means: This tells me what it shows about my topic, so for my experiment I expect what you think will happen, or I will what you will do differently because of it.

Each source needs three things: the APA citation, a summary, and a "What this means" statement. Put one source on each slide.

Part 3. APA citations

Be consistent. Use the citation builder for every source so they all match, and use the same style on your slides and on your References slide. Mixed styles lose points.

APA is a standard way to give credit. You will use two kinds: a short in-text citation on your slide or in your writing, and a full reference on your References slide.

The full reference for a journal article

Last name, A. A., & Last name, B. B. (Year). Title of the article in sentence case. Name of the Journal in Title Case, volume number(issue number), page range. https://doi.org/xxxxx
Made-up example, do not cite it:
Rivera, M. A., & Chen, L. (2019). Slow breathing and resting heart rate in middle school students. Journal of Example Science, 12(3), 45–58. https://doi.org/10.0000/example

The in-text citation

  • One author: (Rivera, 2019)
  • Two authors: (Rivera & Chen, 2019)
  • Three or more authors: (Rivera et al., 2019)
  • In a sentence: Rivera and Chen (2019) found that...

Fix Google's citation using this checklist

  • Authors are last name first, then initials only, not first names.
  • Use & before the last author in the reference. Use "and" in a sentence.
  • The article title is in sentence case: only the first word, the first word after a colon, and names like "American" are capitalized. Google often capitalizes every word, so fix it.
  • The journal name and the volume number are in italics. The article title is not italic and has no quotation marks.
  • Add the DOI link at the end if the article page shows one. It starts with https://doi.org/
  • On your References slide, list sources in alphabetical order by the first author's last name.
  • In Google Docs you can make a hanging indent (the second line is indented) under Format, Align and indent, Indentation options, Special indent.
