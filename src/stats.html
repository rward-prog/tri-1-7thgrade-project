Use percent change, not just raw numbers

Raw data is the actual number you measured, like 120 bpm. The problem is that people start in different places. If your resting heart rate is 60 bpm and your partner's is 90 bpm, a heart rate of 120 bpm means something different for each of you. Percent change shows how much a number changed compared to its own starting number, so everyone is compared fairly.

Percent Change = (After − Before) ÷ Before × 100

How to do it:

  1. Measure a starting number (baseline) before every trial. For heart rate, that is your resting heart rate. For running, do one normal run first.
  2. Measure your ending number after the trial.
  3. Calculate the percent change for each trial. In Google Sheets, type =(C2-B2)/B2 and format the cell as a percent. (See Tables and graphs.)
  4. Average the percent changes for each condition, graph them, and run your p-value on the percent changes.
  5. Keep your raw numbers in your table too: columns for Before, After, and % Change.

Use percent change for heart rate, breaths per minute, running time, and any measurement where each trial has a starting number. A positive percent means the number went up. A negative percent means it went down.

Check the AI. AI can make math mistakes. In Google Sheets, type =T.TEST(A2:A6, B2:B6, 2, 1) with your own cell ranges. The last number is 1 for paired data and 3 for separate groups. Your answers should match.

Before you graph: check that your graph type fits your data, use units on the axes, and Capitalize Important Words in every title. See Tables and graphs.

What a p-value means. It is the chance of seeing a difference this big if the null hypothesis were true (if the condition really did nothing). A p-value below 0.05 means the difference is probably real (statistically significant), so you reject the null hypothesis. Above 0.05 means you cannot be sure it was not luck, so you fail to reject the null hypothesis.
